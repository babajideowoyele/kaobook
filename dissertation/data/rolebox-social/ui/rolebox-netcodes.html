Network Role Categories

Based on watch-complexity influence typology (Miceli, 2018)

High Influence Roles
RoleDescriptionCharacteristics
TopHub The most important hub of the entire network. Main engine of information exchange. Highest influence score, balanced in/out flow
Hub Key node that manages flows and strives for information distribution. High influence, balanced in/out ratio
Megamplifier Listens little but talks too much. Acts as megaphone to connected nodes. Very high out-degree, low in-degree
Amplifier Talks more than listens. Increases the flow of information. High out-degree ratio, moderate connections
Vulcano Spreads huge flow of information without receiving. Super active producer, ignored by incoming flow. Extremely high out-degree, zero/near-zero in-degree
Bridging & Connecting Roles
RoleDescriptionCharacteristics
Bridge Ties together two hubs allowing direct passage of information. High betweenness, connects distinct clusters
Connector Only connects together two common nodes. Moderate betweenness, low clustering
Transceiver Medium or small hub balanced between production and reception. Receives and emits similar amounts. Balanced in/out ratio, moderate degree
Channeler Directs information channeled by a reducer or dam into a chain. Moderate degree, specific flow direction
Chain Link in a directed word-of-mouth chain. Receives and passes to next unique connected node. Exactly one in-link, one out-link
Flow Regulation Roles
RoleDescriptionCharacteristics
Dam Listens too much but talks very little. Acts as information selector or barrier to spread. Very high in-degree, very low out-degree
Reducer Negative regulator reducing the flow of information. Like a Dam but not as strong. High in-degree ratio, moderate activity
Receiver & Peripheral Roles
RoleDescriptionCharacteristics
Receiver Holds only incoming connections. Pure listening role, what it receives does not return. Only in-degree, zero out-degree
Receiver Branch Receives information only from single connected node. Exactly one incoming connection
Blackhole Absorbs huge amount of information and emits nothing. Totally inactive in producing. Very high in-degree, zero out-degree
Idle Passive role, listens a bit and is mute. Low in-degree, zero/near-zero out-degree
Emitter Roles
RoleDescriptionCharacteristics
Emitter Does not listen, only a source of information. Only out-degree, zero in-degree
Emitter Branch Source of information only for the single node to which connected. Exactly one outgoing connection
Low Emitter Speaks little and never listens. Very weak emitter. Low out-degree, zero in-degree

Coding Guidelines

Using Network Metrics

Metrics provide structural hints but require human judgment. High degree alone doesn't mean Hub - consider the quality and diversity of connections. Low clustering with high betweenness suggests Bridge.

Common Mistakes

- Assigning "Hub" to any account with many followers
- Confusing "Amplifier" with anyone who retweets
- Labeling inactive accounts as "Peripheral" without checking history

Best Practices

- Review sample tweets for context
- Consider the actor's apparent purpose/mission
- Check if cross-community mentions suggest Bridge role
- Note uncertainty in coding notes
