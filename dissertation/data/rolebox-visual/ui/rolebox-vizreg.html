Ideational Metafunction: What is represented?

Participants
The people, objects, or entities depicted in the image.
People Types: Academic, Industry Rep, Student, Policymaker, Entrepreneur, Citizen
Technology Types: Lab Equipment, Digital Interface, Prototype, Commercial Product, Infrastructure
Processes
The actions or activities being performed.
Types: Research Work, Collaboration, Presentation, Experimentation, Networking, Demonstration
Circumstances (Settings)
The environment or context where actions take place.
Types: Laboratory, Office, Conference Room, Factory/Production, Natural Environment, Event Venue

Interpersonal Metafunction: What relationship with viewer?

Contact
Whether participants look directly at the viewer (demand) or away (offer).
Strong: Direct eye contact, demanding engagement
Weak: Glance or partial contact
None: Participants absorbed in activity (offer)
Social Distance
Implied closeness based on framing (close-up, medium, long shot).
Intimate: Close-up, personal connection
Interpersonal: Medium shot, social relation
Impersonal: Long shot, public distance
Vertical Angle (Power)
Camera angle suggesting power relationships.
High Angle: Viewer has power over subject
Eye Level: Equality between viewer and subject
Low Angle: Subject has power over viewer
Coding Orientation
The aesthetic code used to establish credibility.
Naturalistic: Photographic realism
Technological: Technical precision
Sensory: Emotional, artistic
Abstract: Diagrammatic, schematic

Textual Metafunction: How is it organized?

Salience
Which elements are made most prominent through size, color, contrast, or position.
People Primary: Humans dominate
Tech Primary: Technology dominates
Setting Primary: Environment dominates
Balanced: No clear hierarchy
Framing
How elements are connected or separated visually.
Strong: Clear boundaries, distinct zones
Weak: Soft transitions between areas
None: Unified composition

Visual Gazes: Characteristic Configurations

Partnership Gaze
Positions viewers as potential partners through direct engagement and egalitarian framing.
Typical Profile: Mixed groups, collaboration activities, interpersonal distance, eye-level angle, naturalistic orientation, weak-to-strong contact.
Impact Gaze
Positions viewers as evaluators through technology-focused, outcome-oriented imagery.
Typical Profile: Technology/infrastructure prominent, impersonal distance, viewer-power angle, technological orientation, minimal contact.
Talent Gaze
Positions viewers as potential participants through aspirational individual portraits.
Typical Profile: Individual focus, presentation/intellectual work, intimate distance, strong direct contact, sensory orientation.

Absence Analysis: What's NOT Shown

Critical visual analysis examines not only what is present but what is systematically absent. This module helps identify gaps between expected representations and actual visual choices. Absences reveal ideological framings and institutional blind spots.
