\chapter{Wikipedia: The Recognized Map}
\label{app:wikipedia}

\noindent\textit{Wikipedia articles emerge from collaborative editing, reflecting how organizations are \emph{recognized}
by external observers---distinct from both their self-declarations and third-party attributions.
This appendix documents the bridging workflow connecting Wikipedia data to role theory constructs,
following the data--theory integration framework of \textcite{maass2018data}.}

\bigskip


%===============================================================================
\section{The Bridging Problem}
%===============================================================================

Collaborative knowledge platforms and role theory address complementary aspects
of organizational identity from different epistemological traditions.
Wikipedia represents how organizations are \emph{perceived} and \emph{categorized}
by diverse contributors---external recognition distinct from the declared
identity of websites (\cref{app:websites}) and attributed roles of news media
(\cref{app:news-media}).
The challenge is bridging these perspectives: using patterns of collaborative
description to operationalize recognition-based constructs, and using
theoretical expectations about role legitimation to guide what features to extract.

\Cref{fig:wikipedia-bridging} shows how the \RoleBox{} Wikipedia pipeline
enacts this bridging workflow.
The upper path (\textit{from patterns to theory}) extracts recognition patterns
from collaborative editing and maps them to role constructs.
The lower path (\textit{from theory to data requirements}) uses role theory
to specify what aspects of Wikipedia content and structure to capture.
The pipeline---crawling, NER, topic modeling, knowledge graph extraction---sits
at the intersection as bridging knowledge.

\section{From Patterns to Theory}
\label{sec:wikipedia-patterns-theory}
%===============================================================================

The upper path in \cref{fig:wikipedia-bridging} traces how collaborative
knowledge production yields role-relevant patterns.
The workflow proceeds through three stages: data collection, pattern extraction,
and theoretical mapping.

\subsection{Stage 1: Data Collection}

We collected Wikipedia and Wikidata content for \textbf{243 entities}---111
NetZeroCities cities and 132 EIT KIC partner organizations---achieving 90\%
coverage (218 articles found).
Cities achieved 100\% Wikipedia coverage; organizations 81\%.
\Cref{fig:wikipedia-collection} summarizes the parallel collection pipeline.

\subsection{Stage 2: Pattern Extraction}

Three families of patterns emerge from Wikipedia content, each capturing
a distinct dimension of external recognition.

\paragraph{Recognition through vocabulary.}
Named Entity Recognition (NER) using spaCy's \texttt{en\_core\_web\_sm}
identified 172,774 entity mentions across the corpus (53,835 for organizations,
118,939 for cities).
These mentions reveal \emph{what organizations are associated with}---not
what organizations claim about themselves (websites) or what journalists
attribute to them (news), but what collaborative editors consider relevant
context.
High co-mention frequency signals perceived affiliation.

\paragraph{Recognition through categorization.}
BERTopic topic modeling discovered latent thematic clusters: 5 topics for
organizations, 7 for cities.
Organizational topics reflect institutional types (universities, companies)
and sectoral positioning.\sidenote{%
    For organizations: Topic 1 ``university, research, students'' (42 docs);
    Topic 2 ``university, college, institute'' (28 docs);
    Topic 3 ``company, industry, technology'' (19 docs).}
City topics capture geographic clustering and urban characteristics.\sidenote{%
    For cities: Topic 0 ``city, amsterdam, munich'' (35 docs);
    Topic 2 ``city, italy, milan'' (22 docs);
    Topic 3 ``city, helsinki, stockholm'' (18 docs).}
Topic assignment reveals how editors \emph{categorize} actors---the
recognized type, not the claimed type.

\paragraph{Recognition through relationships.}
Knowledge graph extraction using kg-gen with Llama~3.2 identified 1,311
unique entities and 1,330 relations from organizational articles, yielding
734 distinct predicates (relation types).
These subject--predicate--object triples map perceived relationships:
``located in,'' ``founded in,'' ``member of.''
The resulting network captures \emph{how editors connect organizations
to institutional contexts}---a collaborative representation of organizational
embeddedness.

Four mappings operationalize external recognition:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm, itemsep=0.3em]
\item[Co-mention frequency $\rightarrow$ Perceived affiliation.]
Organizations frequently mentioned together in Wikipedia articles are
\emph{perceived as connected} by collaborative editors.
This differs from declared partnerships (websites) and attributed
relationships (news)---it captures public recognition of association.

\item[Topic assignment $\rightarrow$ Legitimated sociotype.]
BERTopic clusters reveal how editors \emph{categorize} organizations:
university-focused articles cluster separately from company-focused articles.
This is the recognized sociotype---the category assignment that external
observers consider legitimate and appropriate.

\item[Knowledge graph relations $\rightarrow$ Institutional embedding.]
Extracted triples like ``located in,'' ``member of,'' and ``founded by''
map how editors situate organizations within institutional contexts.
This operationalizes \emph{embeddedness}---not structural position
(network metrics) but relational context (symbolic associations).

\item[Powell Triangle scores $\rightarrow$ Knowledge triangle positioning.]
Keyword-based classification using dictionaries for associational,
scientific, and managerial domains positions each entity on the knowledge
triangle.\sidenote{%
    Organizations: 56\% dominant scientific, 23\% dominant managerial,
    9\% balanced. Cities: 28\% balanced, 36\% mixed, reflecting
    multifaceted urban governance.}
This measures how Wikipedia content emphasizes different integration logics---distinct
from how organizations \emph{claim} positioning (websites).
\end{description}

\section{From Theory to Data Requirements}
\label{sec:wikipedia-theory-data}
%===============================================================================

The lower path in \cref{fig:wikipedia-bridging} runs in reverse:
role theory specifies what aspects of collaborative knowledge to extract.
This is the \emph{theory-to-data-requirements} pathway that
\textcite{maass2018data} identify as essential for bridging.

\paragraph{CAS theory requires relational signals.}
Complex adaptive systems theory predicts that role emergence depends on
mutual recognition---actors claiming roles that others validate.
This requirement guided extraction of entity co-mentions (who is
associated with whom) and knowledge graph predicates (what relationships
editors consider noteworthy).
Wikipedia provides external validation signals: if editors consistently
link two organizations, that linkage constitutes \emph{recognized affiliation}.

\paragraph{Sociotype theory requires categorical boundaries.}
Role constellation theory hypothesizes that sociotypes emerge when
categorical distinctions become salient and stable.
This guided topic modeling (how do editors cluster organizations into types?)
and structured extraction via NuExtract (what organizational attributes
do editors emphasize?).
Wikipedia categorization reveals \emph{legitimated boundaries}---which
distinctions matter enough for editors to encode them.

\paragraph{Knowledge triangle theory requires domain vocabulary.}
The knowledge triangle framework expects organizations to integrate
education, research, and business logics.
This guided construction of Powell Triangle scoring dictionaries and
extraction of domain-specific vocabulary from article sections.
Wikipedia emphasis reveals \emph{public-facing positioning}---how
organizations are understood to relate to the knowledge triangle,
not how they position themselves internally.

\section{The Claim--Recognition Gap}
\label{sec:wikipedia-gap}
%===============================================================================

The most theoretically productive pattern is not a data finding
but a \textbf{bridging gap}: many organizations use vocabulary
associated with innovation and integration on their websites
(\cref{app:websites}), but Wikipedia articles emphasize
institutional type (university, company) over functional role.

This \emph{claim--recognition gap} is not noise---it is signal.
It indicates that external observers prioritize categorical
membership (what \emph{kind} of actor) over functional positioning
(what \emph{role} enacted).
Wikipedia editors default to institutional taxonomies (public/private,
education/business) rather than adopting actors' self-described roles.

\begin{criticalbox}[The Gap as Bridge to Other Modalities]
The claim--recognition gap cannot be resolved within Wikipedia data alone.
Resolving it requires triangulation:
Do organizations \emph{enact} the roles they claim through investment
activity (\cref{app:crunchbase})?
Do news media \emph{attribute} the roles Wikipedia categorizes
(\cref{app:news-media})?
Do visual registers \emph{perform} integration across categories
(\cref{ch:visual-registers})?
Each resolution path connects this modality's workflow to the next,
creating the multimodal cartography that the dissertation argues for.
\end{criticalbox}

\section{Workflow Challenges}
\label{sec:wikipedia-challenges}
%===============================================================================

Wikipedia analysis faces six operational challenges,
each of which shapes what patterns are detectable:

\tableminimal
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3cm}p{4.5cm}p{4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Challenge} & \textbf{Constraint} & \textbf{Consequence} \\
\midrule
Snapshot-only data & No revision tracking yet & Cannot trace recognition evolution \\
QID matching accuracy & Some Wikidata identifiers incorrect & Requires manual verification \\
English-only limitation & Single language edition & Misses cultural framing variation \\
Coverage gaps & Not all orgs have articles & Selection bias toward prominent actors \\
NuExtract model limits & Template-based extraction & Misses non-standard information \\
Collaborative dynamics & Edit wars, POV pushing & Text reflects dominant editor coalition \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\captionof{table}{Wikipedia workflow challenges and consequences}
\end{center}

These challenges are not reasons to dismiss Wikipedia data---they are
\emph{methodological constraints} that bound what claims are warranted.
Future work should address revision history extraction, multilingual
comparison, and editor network analysis to capture recognition dynamics
rather than static snapshots.


%===============================================================================
\section{Summary}
%===============================================================================

\begin{summarybox}
The Wikipedia modality produces the \textbf{recognized map}---how collaborative
knowledge platforms characterize organizations through external categorization.
The bridging workflow connects 243 entities (111 cities, 132 organizations)
to role theory via two paths: entity co-mentions and topic clusters map to
perceived affiliation and legitimated sociotypes; knowledge graph relations
and triangle scores map to institutional embedding and knowledge triangle
positioning.
The most productive finding is not within either path but at their intersection:
the \emph{claim--recognition gap} that motivates multimodal triangulation
across websites, news media, and social platforms.
\end{summarybox}
