\chapter{Crunchbase: The Enacted Map}
\label{app:crunchbase}

\noindent\textit{While websites declare claims (\cref{app:websites}) and news media attributes portrayals,
investment data reveals what organizations \emph{actually do} with resources.
This appendix documents the bridging workflow that connects Crunchbase investment data
to role theory constructs, producing the enacted map of organizational sociotypes.}

\bigskip


%===============================================================================
\section{The Bridging Problem}
%===============================================================================

Investment data and role theory occupy distinct analytical spaces.
On the data side, venture databases like Crunchbase track funding rounds,
investor portfolios, acquisitions, and founder mobility---pre-structured relational
graphs of resource flows.
On the theory side, role theory specifies that organizational roles emerge from
\emph{patterns of interaction}, not declarations alone, and complex adaptive
systems theory predicts that enacted behaviors (not claimed identities) drive
ecosystem dynamics.
The challenge is bridging between these perspectives---using investment patterns
to operationalize enacted roles, and using role theory to guide what investment
features matter.

\Cref{fig:crunchbase-bridging} shows how the \RoleBox{} Crunchbase pipeline
enacts this bridging workflow.
The upper path (\textit{from patterns to theory}) extracts implicit patterns
from investment data and maps them onto role constructs.
The lower path (\textit{from theory to data requirements}) uses role theory
to specify what investment relationships and temporal dynamics to capture.
The pipeline itself---entity matching, network construction, similarity
computation---sits at the intersection as bridging knowledge.

data collection, pattern extraction, and theoretical mapping.

\subsection{Stage 2: Pattern Extraction}

Three families of patterns emerge from investment data, each revealing
different facets of enacted roles.

\paragraph{Resource flow patterns from funding networks.}
Bipartite investor-company networks track resource provision.
Standard network metrics---in-degree (capital received),
out-degree (capital deployed), reciprocity (co-investment patterns)---identify
structural positions corresponding to role archetypes:
high in-degree signals well-resourced startups,
high out-degree signals active investors,
bridge positions signal cross-cluster intermediaries.\footnote{%
    Unlike hyperlink networks (\cref{app:websites}) where edges
    require interpretation, investment edges have unambiguous semantics:
    money moved from investor to startup, creating high-confidence
    relational structures.}

\paragraph{Temporal patterns from funding sequences.}
Time-stamped funding rounds enable longitudinal analysis:
time-to-milestone progression (seed to Series A),
funding wave detection (boom-bust cycles),
investor entry/exit timing.
\textcite{vandenheuvel2022cleantech} demonstrated this with cleantech VC:
funding spiked to 10\% of total VC in 2008, then crashed to 3\% by 2015,
revealing sector-specific boom-bust dynamics invisible in static snapshots.

\paragraph{Semantic patterns from company descriptions.}
Crunchbase provides short textual descriptions (typically 50--200 words)
of what companies do.
Topic modeling and embedding analysis reveal how startups \emph{signal}
their positioning to investors.
\textcite{yang2024sustainability} found that sustainability ventures cluster
into distinct narrative types: ``cleantech,'' ``social impact,''
``circular economy,'' each attracting different investor profiles.

Four mappings deserve emphasis:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm, itemsep=0.3em]
\item[Network position $\rightarrow$ Resource role.]
High out-degree maps to \emph{resource provider} (active investor);
high in-degree to \emph{well-resourced recipient} (heavily funded startup);
high betweenness to \emph{intermediary} or \emph{bridge investor};
peripheral position to \emph{niche actor} or \emph{isolated venture}.
These structural roles operationalize the resource flow mechanisms
from \cref{ch:cas}.

\item[Co-investment clusters $\rightarrow$ Alliance constellations.]
Companies co-funded by the same investors form \emph{portfolio families}.
These clusters reveal enacted alliance structures---not claimed partnerships
(\cref{app:websites}) but observable resource co-allocation.
The RoleSim integration (\cref{sec:crunchbase-rolesim}) classifies
organizations into 22 influence types based on these patterns.

\item[Funding stage sequence $\rightarrow$ Lifecycle positioning.]
Seed/Series A/B/C/Late stage classifications map directly to
venture lifecycle theory.
Time-to-progression metrics operationalize \textcite{nathan2017crunchbase}'s
``venture maturation'' construct, distinguishing fast-growing rockets from
slow-burn ventures.

\item[Description vocabulary $\rightarrow$ Enacted sociotype.]
Unlike website text (curated for broad audiences), Crunchbase descriptions
target \emph{investors}.
The vocabulary profile reveals how organizations position themselves
in capital markets---the \emph{enacted sociotype} complementing
the claimed sociotype from \cref{app:websites}.
\end{description}


%===============================================================================
\section{From Theory to Data Requirements}
\label{sec:crunchbase-theory-data}
%===============================================================================

The lower path in \cref{fig:crunchbase-bridging} runs in reverse:
role theory specifies what to extract from investment data.
This is the \emph{theory-to-data-requirements} pathway that
\textcite{maass2018data} identify as essential for bridging.

\paragraph{CAS theory requires temporal dynamics.}
Complex adaptive systems theory predicts that roles evolve through
interaction sequences, not static positions.
This requirement guided the extraction of time-stamped funding rounds
(enabling temporal network analysis) and founder mobility tracking
(enabling human capital flow mapping).
Static snapshots miss the dynamics central to CAS emergence.

\paragraph{Knowledge triangle theory requires multi-sector coverage.}
The knowledge triangle framework hypothesizes distinct roles for
education, research, and business actors.
This guided the construction of EIT KIC sector mappings:
eight sectors with configurable category keywords and multi-attribute
similarity computation across sector, geography, funding stage,
and investor overlap (seven dimensions total).

\paragraph{Role constellation theory requires ecosystem-wide networks.}
The theoretical expectation of \emph{constellations}---complementary roles
interacting together---required building multi-layer networks:
investor-company bipartite graphs,
co-investor one-mode projections,
founder mobility interlocks,
and company similarity networks.
Near-complete coverage (48\% of 2.8M total Crunchbase organizations
classified as EIT-relevant) enables constellation mapping across
the full innovation ecosystem.

\section{The Claim--Action Gap}
\label{sec:crunchbase-gap}
%===============================================================================

The most theoretically productive finding is not within either bridging path
but at their intersection: the \textbf{claim--action gap} between
declared sociotypes (\cref{app:websites}) and enacted sociotypes
(investment data).

Organizations claiming ``sustainability leadership'' may receive funding
primarily from traditional VC, not impact investors.
Companies declaring ``research collaboration'' may have no academic investors
or university co-founders.
Startups proclaiming ``market disruption'' may remain in seed stage for years,
never progressing to growth funding.

This gap is not noise---it is signal.
It indicates:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Aspirational positioning}: claiming sought identities before enacting them
\item \textbf{Strategic ambiguity}: broad claims to attract diverse investors
\item \textbf{Market constraints}: enacted roles shaped by capital availability, not choice
\item \textbf{Temporal lag}: current investment reflects past positioning
\end{itemize}

\begin{criticalbox}[The Gap as Bridge to Other Modalities]
The claim--action gap cannot be resolved within investment data alone.
Resolving it requires triangulation:
Does news media portray the claimed or enacted identity (\cref{app:news-media})?
Do social media networks confirm investor relationships (\cref{app:social-media})?
Does visual co-occurrence show enacted partnerships (\cref{app:visual-materials})?
Each resolution path connects this modality's workflow to the next,
creating the multimodal cartography that the dissertation argues for.
\end{criticalbox}

\section{Workflow Challenges}
\label{sec:crunchbase-challenges}
%===============================================================================

Investment data analysis faces six workflow challenges,
each motivating specific methodological responses:

\subsection{API Licensing Constraints}

Crunchbase shifted to a commercial API model post-2016, restricting
data access and posing reproducibility challenges.\footnote{%
    The Academic Research dataset used here is a 2023 snapshot,
    not real-time API access, trading currency for reproducibility.}
Research workflows must balance open science principles against
proprietary data constraints.

\subsection{Success and Survivorship Bias}

Companies update profiles strategically, especially after successful funding.
Failed ventures often abandon their profiles, creating
\emph{survivorship bias}---over-representing successful exits
and under-representing failures.\cite{kauffman2017crunchbase}
Network analysis on survived-only samples yields
upward-biased centrality metrics.

\subsection{Geographic Coverage Gaps}

Coverage is strongest in US and major startup hubs
(Silicon Valley, London, Singapore).
Emerging economies and peripheral regions remain under-represented,
creating \emph{geographic bias} that skews ecosystem mapping
toward established innovation centers.\cite{nathan2017crunchbase}

\subsection{Entity Matching Across Data Sources}

Matching Crunchbase organizations to external datasets (patents, publications,
social media) requires fuzzy name matching and domain resolution.
Missing fields (31\% lack location data in some samples) complicate
cross-dataset integration.\cite{nathan2017crunchbase}
The \texttt{rolebox-db} schema implements entity linking across
eight data sources to address this challenge.

\subsection{Backfilling and Temporal Accuracy}

Funding rounds are often backfilled months after occurrence,
introducing \emph{temporal lag} between event and record.
``Selection bias towards the most successful firms easiest to find info about''
means early-stage or struggling ventures have sparser, less reliable data.%
\cite{kauffman2017crunchbase}

\subsection{Private Investment Opacity}

Small angel rounds, family office investments, and government grants
frequently go unreported.
What appears as ``low in-degree'' (under-resourced) may reflect
\emph{reporting gaps} rather than funding scarcity.

\section{RoleSim Integration: Influence Typology}
\label{sec:crunchbase-rolesim}
%===============================================================================

A methodologically significant extension is integration with the
\textbf{RoleSim} influence typology framework, which classifies
organizations into \textbf{22 role types} based on network position metrics.

\subsection{Network Construction}

The \texttt{crunchbase\_rolesim.py} module constructs four network types
from investment data:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm, itemsep=0.3em]
\item[Investor-Company Bipartite.]
Directed edges from investors to funded companies,
weighted by investment amount and round count.
Captures resource flow directionality.

\item[Co-Investor Network.]
One-mode projection connecting investors who co-invested in shared deals.
Edge weight reflects number of shared portfolio companies
(minimum threshold: 2 shared deals).
Reveals investment consortia and alliance patterns.

\item[Company Similarity Network.]
Companies connected based on multi-attribute similarity:
KIC overlap (25\%), industry categories (20\%), geography (15\%),
funding stage (15\%), company scale (10\%), founding year (10\%),
and investor overlap (5\%).
Edges above configurable similarity threshold (default: 0.10).

\item[Founder Mobility Network.]
Companies connected through shared founders, executives, or board members.
Captures human capital flow and entrepreneurial recombination.
\end{description}

\subsection{Influence Role Classification}

Each organization is classified into one of 22 influence types
based on network position:

\section{Multi-Attribute Similarity and Embeddings}
\label{sec:crunchbase-similarity}
%===============================================================================

Beyond network topology, Crunchbase enables semantic similarity analysis
through dual representation:

\paragraph{TF-IDF sparse vectors.}
Traditional term frequency-inverse document frequency with configurable
parameters (max features: 1000, n-gram range: 1--2, min/max document
frequency thresholds).
Produces sparse vectors for keyword-based similarity.

\paragraph{Sentence embeddings.}
SBERT models (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, all-mpnet-base-v2) produce dense
384/768-dimensional vectors capturing semantic similarity beyond
keyword matching.
The \texttt{MethodComparison} class quantifies agreement via
Adjusted Rand Index, Normalized Mutual Information, and
similarity matrix correlation.

\paragraph{Aggregate similarity networks.}
Combining text embeddings with structural attributes
(sector, geography, funding stage, investor overlap) produces
multi-dimensional similarity space.
\Cref{fig:crunchbase-network} visualizes the aggregate similarity network
for EIT-relevant organizations, with nodes colored by primary KIC sector
and edges above similarity threshold 0.15.

%===============================================================================
\section{Cross-Modal Triangulation}
\label{sec:crunchbase-triangulation}
%===============================================================================

The \texttt{cross\_modal\_triangulation.py} module implements systematic
comparison across data modalities, operationalizing the dissertation's
core argument that roles are multiply constituted.

\subsection{The Three Maps}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm, itemsep=0.4em]
\item[Declared Map (\cref{app:websites}).]
What organizations claim to be.
Vector representations from website text capture self-positioning strategies.

\item[Attributed Map (\cref{app:news-media}).]
How organizations are portrayed by external observers.
News media analysis reveals socially-constructed reputation.

\item[Enacted Map (Crunchbase).]
What organizations actually do with resources.
Investment patterns reveal observable behavior.
\end{description}

\subsection{Alignment Detection}

Organizations are classified into alignment categories based on
cross-modal similarity:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0.2em]
\item \textbf{Aligned} ($>$70\% average similarity): Consistent identity across modalities
\item \textbf{Partial} (40--70\%): Some modality pairs align while others diverge
\item \textbf{Divergent} ($<$40\%): Significant gaps between modalities
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Gap Typology}

The module detects three gap types, each theoretically informative:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0.5cm, itemsep=0.3em]
\item[Claim-Action Gap.]
Website declarations diverge from investment behavior.
May indicate aspirational positioning, strategic ambiguity,
or market constraints shaping enacted roles.

\item[Claim-Perception Gap.]
Website declarations diverge from media portrayal.
External observers see the organization differently than
it presents itself---a legitimacy challenge.

\item[Perception-Action Gap.]
Media portrayal diverges from investment behavior.
May indicate reputational lag (media slow to update)
or disconnected narratives (media hype without funding substance).
\end{description}

\begin{insightbox}[Gaps as Analytical Signal]
The cross-modal triangulation workflow treats gaps not as measurement error
but as \emph{substantive findings}.
Divergence reveals the multiple constitution of organizational roles:
claimed, attributed, and enacted identities may be partially independent,
reflecting the multi-faceted nature of role emergence in complex systems.
\end{insightbox}


%===============================================================================
\section{Summary}
%===============================================================================

\begin{summarybox}
The Crunchbase modality produces the \textbf{enacted map}---what organizations
actually do with resources, complementing declared claims and attributed portrayals.
The bridging workflow connects 1.35M investment records to role theory via
two paths: network position patterns map to resource flow roles,
and funding sequences map to lifecycle positioning.
The most productive finding is not within either path but at their intersection:
the \emph{claim--action gap} that motivates multimodal triangulation.

Key methodological contributions:
RoleSim integration (22 influence types),
multi-attribute similarity (7 dimensions),
cross-modal alignment detection,
and workflow challenge responses (survivorship bias, geographic gaps,
entity matching, backfilling lag).
The enacted map reveals that organizational roles are not just claimed or attributed---they
are \emph{performed} through observable resource allocation,
making investment data essential for role cartography in innovation ecosystems.
\end{summarybox}
